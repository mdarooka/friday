A Friday field guide

Coast, hills,
one island.

A starting point from Friday’s Sri Lanka catalogue. Roads are slower than the map suggests, so a good trip picks two or three bases. Sample catalogue, not live inventory.

How many days do you need in Sri Lanka?

Start with the days you have.

Friday’s Sri Lanka planner offers three lengths: an unhurried week, a ten-day loop, or about fourteen days. They are prompts, not packages. The catalogue keeps most days to one area, with a long drive on the days you change base.

When is the best time to visit Sri Lanka?

The two coasts do not peak together.

The catalogue says the island has two monsoons, so the coast and the hills are not at their best at the same time. The south-west coast is generally best from December to March, and the east coast from about April to September. Whale watching off Mirissa is described as generally best from November to April. Elephant gatherings at the Minneriya reservoir are a dry-season note, about July to October. This guide does not confirm live weather.

Where to go

Which places fit
one Sri Lanka trip?

These are places from Friday’s hand-researched destination catalogue. Ratings and review counts in that catalogue are sample data, not live figures. A designer confirms what can be held.

Colombo

Colombo

A first or last night: Gangaramaya beside Beira Lake, the National Museum, and Galle Face Green at sunset.

  • Gangaramaya Temple — A busy, eclectic temple beside Beira Lake, full of gifts, statues and a small museum. Go in the cool of the morning.
  • Colombo National Museum — The island's main museum, with the Kandyan throne and fine masks. A good indoor hour.
  • Galle Face Green — A long ocean-front lawn where Colombo flies kites and eats isso vadai at sunset.

Galle

Galle Fort

The fort’s ramparts, a lighthouse on the southern tip, and the beaches at Hikkaduwa and Unawatuna.

  • Galle Fort — A seventeenth-century Dutch fort with walkable ramparts, cafes and small galleries. Best in the late afternoon.
  • Galle Lighthouse — A white lighthouse on the fort's southern tip, with sea on three sides.
  • Hikkaduwa Beach — A long surf and snorkelling beach with turtles near the shore. Busy but lively.

Mirissa

Mirissa

A morning boat for blue whales and dolphins, described as generally best from November to April.

  • Mirissa Beach — A wide crescent of sand with palm trees and shacks. A good place to slow down.
  • Coconut Tree Hill — A small headland of tall palms above the sea. Go early or at sunset and share it with fewer people.
  • Mirissa Whale Watching — A morning boat trip for blue whales and dolphins, generally best from November to April. Take a seasickness tablet.

Kandy

Kandy

The Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic, beside Kandy Lake. The catalogue suggests the evening ritual, and modest clothing.

  • Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic — The island's most sacred Buddhist temple, beside Kandy Lake. Go for the evening ritual and wear modest clothing.
  • Royal Botanic Gardens, Peradeniya — A large botanical garden in a loop of the Mahaweli river, with orchids, spice trees and giant bamboo.
  • Ceylon Tea Museum — A tea factory turned museum on the hill above Kandy. See how tea is made, then taste it.

Nuwara Eliya

Nuwara Eliya

Tea country. The catalogue treats it as a highland base, not a day trip from the beach.

  • Nanu Oya Railway Station — The station for Nuwara Eliya on the famous hill-country line. From here, take a tuk-tuk up the hill.
  • Lover's Leap Falls — A slender waterfall reached by a walk through tea bushes. Easy and pretty in the morning.
  • Pedro Tea Estate — A working tea estate with a small factory tour and a cup of fresh tea at the end.

Ella

Ella

A hill base of its own, with the slow roads the planner warns about between bases.

  • Ella Railway Station — A small station on the Kandy to Badulla line. Riding the train from Nanu Oya is a highlight of the hills.
  • Nine Arch Bridge — A stone viaduct in the jungle, built a century ago. Arrive before a train passes and keep your distance from the track.
  • Little Adam's Peak — An easy 45-minute climb through tea bushes to wide views. A good sunrise walk.

Cultural Triangle

Sigiriya

Sigiriya Rock, climbed at opening time, and Minneriya, where elephants gather in the dry season, about July to October.

  • Sigiriya Rock Fortress — A fifth-century palace on a 200 m rock, with frescoes and mirror wall. Climb at opening time, before the heat.
  • Pidurangala Rock — A cheaper, quieter climb with the best view of Sigiriya itself. Go for sunset.
  • Dambulla Cave Temple — Five caves with over a hundred Buddha images and painted ceilings. Take off your shoes and hat at the entrance.

Yala

Udawalawe and Yala

Udawalawe is open grassland the catalogue associates with elephants. Yala is the best-known park for leopards, and the note there is a crowd of jeeps at dawn.

  • Udawalawe National Park — Open grassland with a very high chance of seeing elephants. A good first safari, especially with children.
  • Yala National Park — The island's best-known park for leopards, elephants and crocodiles. Go at dawn and be ready for a crowd of jeeps.
  • Kataragama Temple — A shared shrine for Hindus, Buddhists and Muslims. The evening puja is worth a short visit.

Trincomalee

Trincomalee

The east coast, which the catalogue puts at its better months from about April to September.

  • Koneswaram Temple — A hilltop Shiva temple above the sea, with a statue of the god and sweeping bay views.
  • Pigeon Island National Park — A small coral island with clear water and reef sharks near the shore. Best snorkelled in the calm months.
  • Nilaveli Beach — A long, quiet white-sand beach on the north-east coast, with calm water from about April to September.

Arugam Bay

Arugam Bay

A surf bay on the south-east. The catalogue puts that season at roughly May to September.

  • Arugam Bay — A surf bay on the south-east coast. The season runs roughly from May to September.

Getting there and around

Short on the map, slow on the road.

The catalogue’s planning note is that distances look short and the roads are slow and winding, so a change of base is the long drive of the day.

Pick the coast that matches the month. The south-west is the December to March side; the east is about April to September.

Sample route shapes

How the planner’s lengths can be laid out.

These follow the catalogue’s three lengths. They are shapes, not bookings.

About 7 days

One coast

A week on the south-west in the dry months: Colombo if you need a first night, then Galle and Mirissa. Leave the hills for a longer trip.

About 10 days

Coast and one hill base

South coast, then one of Kandy, Nuwara Eliya or Ella. Do not try to add Sigiriya in the same ten days unless you are happy with the drives.

About 14 days

Two or three bases

South or east coast, the hills, and the Cultural Triangle, with a day for each move. The east-coast months and the south-west months are not the same.

Bases

South coast, hills, or the Cultural Triangle?

The catalogue does not rank them. Galle Fort is a walkable Dutch fort for late afternoon. The tea hills sit at Nuwara Eliya and Ella. Sigiriya anchors the Cultural Triangle. A short trip chooses one of those, not all three. Sample listings are not live booking details.

Budget

What does a Sri Lanka trip cost?

This catalogue does not provide a current, trip-wide cost. Sample place data is not a live quote. Share dates and pace before anyone prices the trip.

Quick answers

Questions the catalogue already answers.

Can I see the whole island in a week?

The catalogue says no. Roads are slow, and a good trip picks two or three bases.

When is the south coast easier?

December to March, in the catalogue’s monsoon note. The east coast is the other half of the year.

Is whale watching a sure thing?

No. The Mirissa boat is described as generally best from November to April. It is not a confirmed departure.

Are the prices in the catalogue live?

No. Ratings, review counts and any sample figures are not a quote. A designer confirms what can be held.
