A Friday field guide

Leh first.
The high lakes later.

From Friday’s Ladakh catalogue. Leh is the arrival area, at about 3,500 m, and the first day is a rest. Nubra, Pangong and Tso Moriri wait until after day three. Sample catalogue, not live inventory.

How many days do you need in Ladakh?

Give the altitude the first days.

The catalogue’s lengths are about five days, an unhurried week, or about ten days. Five is described as the point where the rhythm can settle. Day one has no sightseeing on purpose. The high passes and Pangong come after at least three nights in Leh.

When are the roads open?

About June to mid-September.

The catalogue says Ladakh’s roads are generally open from about June to mid-September. Outside that window the route has to change. July and August are when Nubra and Pangong camps are scarce. This guide does not confirm this year’s road status.

Where to go

Where the catalogue
sends you, and when.

These are places from Friday’s hand-researched destination catalogue. Ratings and review counts in that catalogue are sample data, not live figures. A designer confirms what can be held.

Leh

Leh

The arrival area. Leh Palace above the old town, Shanti Stupa for sunset, the Main Bazaar for a slow walk. The first day stays here, at about 3,500 m.

  • Leh Palace — Nine-storey royal palace above the old town. Go early, when the light is soft and the rooftops are quiet.
  • Shanti Stupa — A white-domed peace stupa above Leh. Sunset is the gentle way to see the valley from above.
  • Namgyal Tsemo Monastery — A small red gompa on the ridge behind the palace, with a wide view over Leh and the Indus valley.

Indus Valley

Indus Valley

Shey, Thiksey (morning prayers at about 7am), Hemis in a side valley, and Stok across the river. Still the acclimatising days, not a pass.

  • Shey Palace and Monastery — The former summer capital of Ladakh's kings, with a great gilded Buddha and a quiet ruined fort above.
  • Thiksey Monastery — A twelve-storey gompa that looks like a small Potala. Morning prayers at about 7am are worth the early start.
  • Hemis Monastery — Ladakh's largest and richest monastery, tucked into a side valley. The museum holds fine thangkas.

Sham Valley

Sham Valley

Alchi’s eleventh-century paintings, low enough to be easy on the body. Lamayuru is the long drive, so it anchors the day. The Indus–Zanskar confluence is a pause, not a destination of its own.

  • Magnetic Hill — A short stop on the Srinagar road where cars seem to roll uphill. Best treated as a fun pause on the way west.
  • Indus and Zanskar Confluence — Where the green Zanskar meets the milky-grey Indus below Nimmu. Rafting starts nearby in summer.
  • Alchi Monastery — An eleventh-century monastery with some of the region's oldest wall paintings. It sits low, so it is easy on the body.

Nubra Valley

Nubra Valley

A high area, after day three. Khardung La is 5,359 m: the catalogue says stay no longer than twenty minutes at the top. Diskit’s Maitreya looks down the Shyok. Hunder is the dune field. Turtuk needs its own day.

  • Khardung La — A 5,359 m pass on the road to Nubra. Stay no longer than twenty minutes at the top and keep sipping water.
  • Diskit Monastery — Nubra's oldest gompa, with a 32 m Maitreya Buddha looking down the Shyok valley.
  • Hunder Sand Dunes — A cold-desert dune field with double-humped Bactrian camels and snow peaks behind. Golden at sunset.

Pangong Tso

Pangong Tso

A high area, after at least three nights in Leh. The lake turns from turquoise to deep blue through the day. Bring warm layers.

  • Pangong Tso — A long salt lake that turns from turquoise to deep blue and violet through the day. Bring warm layers.

Tso Moriri

Tso Moriri

A high area, about 4,500 m, after day three. Korzok is a small gompa on the shore. Tso Kar is the white-rimmed salt lake on the way.

  • Tso Moriri — A high, quiet lake at about 4,500 m, ringed by grassland where kiang and marmots graze.
  • Korzok Monastery — A small gompa on the shore of Tso Moriri, in one of the highest settlements in the region.
  • Tso Kar — A white-rimmed salt lake on the high plateau. Good for birds and for a long silence.

Acclimatisation

The first three nights decide the rest.

Leh is the arrival area. Day one is a rest, with gentle stops only. The catalogue puts Leh at about 3,500 m.

Nubra, Pangong and Tso Moriri are the high areas, and they wait until after day three. A lakes trip is written as three nights around Leh before Pangong.

On the road

Passes, permits, camps.

  • Khardung La is listed at 5,359 m. Stay no longer than twenty minutes at the top, and keep sipping water.
  • The catalogue says it cannot reserve or pay. Camps, permits and drivers need the provider, not this page.
  • Nubra and Pangong nights are marked as camps in the planner, and July and August are when rooms are scarce.
  • Roads are generally open from about June to mid-September. Outside that, change the dates or the route.

Sample route shapes

How the lengths sit with the altitude.

Shapes from the catalogue. The high valleys do not move forward to fill an empty day.

About 5 days

Leh, Indus, Sham

A rest day in Leh, then Thiksey, Hemis and Shey, then Alchi or Lamayuru. No Nubra and no Pangong. Five days is the gentle start, not a lake trip.

About 7 days

One high valley

Three nights around Leh, Indus and Sham, then one of Nubra or Pangong, not both. Come back to Leh before you fly.

About 10 days

Valleys, then the lakes

The week above, plus the second high area only after the third night in Leh. Tso Moriri is the quieter, higher lake, at about 4,500 m, and it needs the same wait.

Valleys

Indus and Sham first, or the high lakes?

The high areas in the catalogue are Nubra, Pangong and Tso Moriri, and they wait until after day three. Before that, Leh, the Indus gompas and Sham (Alchi sits low, which the catalogue calls easy on the body). Pangong is a long salt lake; Tso Moriri is described at about 4,500 m. Do not open with either.

Quick answers

Questions the catalogue already answers.

Can I go to Pangong on day two?

No. The catalogue holds Pangong until after at least three nights in Leh. Nubra and Tso Moriri use the same rule.

How high is Leh?

About 3,500 m, which is why day one has no sightseeing.

When should I go?

Roads are generally open from about June to mid-September. The catalogue does not treat that as a guarantee for a given week.
