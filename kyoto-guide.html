A Friday field guide

Autumn, one
neighbourhood a day.

From Friday’s Kyoto catalogue. Autumn colour generally peaks from mid-November to early December. This is sample catalogue data, not a live plan. Kyoto is not offered as a new trip in the planner; ask a designer.

How many days do you need in Kyoto in autumn?

One area a day.

The catalogue keeps each day to one area, because the sights are spread out and buses are slow when the temples are busy. The lengths it offers are about five days, a week, or ten days. They are prompts. Famous temples go first thing in the morning.

When do the maples turn?

Mid-November into early December.

The catalogue says autumn colour generally peaks from mid-November to early December, a little earlier in the northern hills and later in the centre. Weekends are crowded. Kiyomizu-dera has an autumn evening opening. Kodai-ji’s evening illumination is popular. Nijo Castle’s garden is at its best in mid-November. Tenryu-ji’s maples turn in mid-to-late November. Kitano Tenmangu turns from late November. This guide does not confirm this year’s forecast.

Where to go

Which neighbourhood
for which day?

These are places from Friday’s hand-researched destination catalogue. Ratings and review counts in that catalogue are sample data, not live figures. A designer confirms what can be held. Stays are left off this page so a sample hotel rate is not read as a price.

Southern Higashiyama

Kiyomizu

Kiyomizu-dera’s stage over the maples, with an autumn evening opening. Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka lead down from it. Kodai-ji’s evening illumination is popular.

  • Kiyomizu-dera — A hillside temple with a wooden stage over the maples. Go at opening time, or stay for the autumn evening opening.
  • Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka — Two stone-paved lanes of tea houses and craft shops that lead down from Kiyomizu-dera. Best early or late.
  • Kodai-ji — A quiet Zen temple with a raked-gravel garden and bamboo grove. Its autumn evening illumination is popular.

Gion

Gion

Hanamikoji’s wooden machiya, Yasaka Shrine lit after dark, and Pontocho beside the river. Be quiet if you see a maiko.

  • Hanamikoji Street — The main street of Gion, with wooden machiya and tea houses. Be quiet and respectful if you see a maiko.
  • Yasaka Shrine — A bright vermilion shrine at the east end of Shijo, lit up after dark and open all night.
  • Chion-in — A huge temple complex with a great gate and quiet gardens on the hill behind Maruyama Park.

Central Kyoto

Central Kyoto

Nishiki Market before the crowds, and Nijo Castle, whose garden the catalogue puts at its best in mid-November.

  • Nishiki Market — A five-block covered market of pickles, tofu, sweets and street snacks. Go at 10am, before the crowds.
  • Nijo Castle — The shogun's Kyoto residence, with nightingale floors and painted screens. Its garden is at its best in mid-November.
  • Kyoto Imperial Palace — The old imperial residence, set inside a large gravel park. Entry is free, and you can walk the grounds at leisure.

Arashiyama

Arashiyama

Tenryu-ji’s pond garden, with maples in mid-to-late November. The bamboo grove is the same day’s walk, not a second base.

  • Arashiyama Bamboo Grove — A short path between tall bamboo stems. Go before 8am, when the light is soft and the path is quiet.
  • Tenryu-ji — A Zen temple with a pond garden that has barely changed in seven centuries. Its maples turn in mid-to-late November.
  • Togetsukyo Bridge — A long wooden bridge over the Katsura river, with a hillside of maples behind it.

North-west Kyoto

Kinkaku-ji

Kinkaku-ji at opening time. Kitano Tenmangu’s maples turn from late November. Ryoan-ji is the rock garden, sat on the veranda.

  • Kinkaku-ji — The gold-leafed pavilion beside its mirror pond. It is crowded, so arrive at opening time.
  • Ryoan-ji — Home to Japan's best-known rock garden. Sit on the veranda and let the quiet arrive.
  • Kitano Tenmangu — A shrine with a plum grove and monthly market. Its maples turn from late November.

Northern Higashiyama

Philosopher's Path

Ginkaku-ji and the Philosopher’s Path. Eikan-do has a night opening in autumn. Nanzen-ji’s maples turn late in November.

  • Ginkaku-ji — The Silver Pavilion and its moss garden, quiet and beautiful in autumn. Start here and walk south.
  • Philosopher's Path — A canal-side path lined with cherry and maple trees. Slow and pleasant, with little cafes along the way.
  • Nanzen-ji — A grand Zen temple with a brick aqueduct in its grounds. Its maples turn late in November.

Fushimi

Fushimi

Fushimi Inari’s gates, early or at dusk. Tofuku-ji is the catalogue’s most famous autumn temple, with maples under the Tsutenkyo bridge. Arrive at opening time.

  • Fushimi Inari Taisha — Thousands of vermilion gates on a wooded mountain, open all day. Go in the early morning or at dusk.
  • Tofuku-ji — Kyoto's most famous autumn temple, with a sea of maples beneath the Tsutenkyo bridge. Arrive at opening time.
  • Daigo-ji — A large temple complex in the hills east of the city, with a five-storey pagoda and a quiet garden.

Ohara and Kurama

Kurama and Kibune

The northern hills. Sanzen-in’s moss garden is described as soft autumn colour, understated rather than a peak-week crush.

  • Sanzen-in — A moss-covered temple garden in the hills of Ohara. Its autumn colour is soft and understated.
  • Kurama-dera — A mountain temple reached by a forest trail. The hike to Kibune is best done in daylight.
  • Kibune Shrine — A riverside shrine at the end of the Kurama trail, with lanterns along a stone stair.

Uji

Uji

Tea country south of the city. Byodo-in’s phoenix hall is the catalogue’s reason to make the trip.

  • Byodo-in — The Phoenix Hall from 1053, shown on the ten-yen coin. Quiet in the morning.
  • Ujigami Shrine — Japan's oldest surviving shrine building, in a quiet wood across the river. Few visitors.
  • Nakamura Tokichi Honten — A matcha specialist since 1854, with soba, parfaits and sweets. Expect a queue at midday.

Getting around

Walk a neighbourhood. Do not cross the city twice.

The catalogue’s rule is one area a day. Buses are slow at busy times, and the famous temples are busiest from mid-morning.

If your month is earlier or later than the mid-November to early-December peak, the catalogue says to shift the order rather than assume every temple is at its colour.

Sample route shapes

Autumn, without collecting every temple.

Shapes from the catalogue’s lengths. Not bookings, and not a promise of this year’s foliage dates.

About 5 days

East bank only

Southern Higashiyama, Gion, and Northern Higashiyama. Kiyomizu at opening or for the evening opening. Leave Arashiyama and Fushimi if the days are short.

About 7 days

Add the west and the river

The east-bank days, then Arashiyama for Tenryu-ji, and a morning at Fushimi Inari. Central Kyoto, including Nijo’s mid-November garden, fits one day.

About 10 days

Hills and Uji

The week above, plus the north-west (Kinkaku-ji at opening, Kitano Tenmangu if you are here late in November), a day toward Ohara, and Uji for tea.

Autumn bases

Higashiyama or Arashiyama for the maples?

The catalogue does not choose for you. Southern Higashiyama has Kiyomizu-dera’s wooden stage over the maples. Arashiyama’s Tenryu-ji turns in mid-to-late November. Northern Higashiyama has Eikan-do, which the catalogue calls Kyoto’s best autumn temple, with a night opening, and Nanzen-ji, whose maples turn late in November. Pick one neighbourhood a day.

Budget

What does an autumn week cost?

The catalogue does not give a trip-wide cost. It does say the best kaiseki and ryokan dinners book out weeks ahead in November. Sample figures are not a quote.

Quick answers

Questions the catalogue already answers.

Can I start a Kyoto trip in the planner?

Not as a preset. Kyoto stays off the new-trip list so older saved trips still open, and a new plan does not pretend the city is bookable from the menu. Use Call me back or a quote, or open a blank plan and tell the designer.

When should I book the important meals?

The catalogue says the best kaiseki and ryokan dinners book out weeks ahead in November. Markets and casual soba or udon are the easier meals.

Which temple for late November?

Kitano Tenmangu from late November, and Nanzen-ji late in the month. Tenryu-ji is mid-to-late November. The peak for the famous gardens is still described as mid-November to early December.

No. The catalogue is sample data. A designer confirms what can be held.
