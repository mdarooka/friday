A Friday field guide

A slower way
along the coast.

A practical starting point for a Goa trip: choose a base, leave room for one short outing a day, and decide whether heritage, beach quiet or tables matter most.

How many days do you need in Goa?

Start with the days you have.

Friday’s Goa planner uses three starting points: a short coastal escape, an unhurried week, or a longer stay with room to wander. They are prompts, not fixed packages. For fewer days, pick one stretch of coast rather than trying to join north, south and inland stops in one loop.

When is the best time to visit Goa?

Match the season to the trip.

Official tourism guidance and climate normals point to the dry winter months as the most comfortable stretch for beaches and open-air dining; monsoon periods bring heavy rain and many beach shacks close. Festival weeks and the Christmas–New Year stretch are busier and often more expensive. Check current weather and local operating guidance before booking; this guide does not confirm live conditions.

Where to go

Which places fit
one Goa trip?

These are places from Friday’s hand-researched destination catalogue. Pick a few around the experience you want; check routes and opening details for your own dates.

Panaji

Panjim

For heritage walking: the Fontainhas lanes, the main-square church, markets and river views.

  • Fontainhas (quarter) — Panjim's Latin Quarter: pastel Portuguese-era houses, tiled facades and narrow lanes made for walking.
  • The Black Sheep Bistro — Goan-forward plates and a thoughtful cocktail list in a relaxed Panjim dining room.
  • Cantare — Contemporary Goan cooking with a strong seafood section and calm, unhurried service.

Old Goa

Old Goa

For the UNESCO-listed churches and a quiet museum morning, set apart from the beach coast.

  • Basilica of Bom Jesus — UNESCO-listed baroque basilica that holds the remains of St Francis Xavier.
  • Sé Cathedral — One of Asia's largest churches, with the Golden Bell in its tower.
  • Archaeological Museum and Portrait Gallery — Cool galleries of sculpture and viceroy portraits, beside the basilica.

Calangute

Candolim

For first visits: wide beaches, many tables and short drives, from busy Baga to calmer Candolim.

  • Baga Beach — The liveliest strip of the north coast: shacks by day, music by night.
  • Calangute Beach — Long and busy, with easy loungers and water sports at the northern end.
  • Candolim Beach — Calmer than Calangute, with wide sand and unhurried shacks.

Anjuna

Anjuna

For cafés, village lanes and markets, with a food-led, more relaxed feel.

  • Artjuna — A leafy garden café in Anjuna known for its bowls, coffee and slow breakfasts.
  • Anjuna Beach — Red laterite cliffs, sunset sessions and a relaxed shack scene.
  • Anjuna Flea Market — The Wednesday flea market: textiles, jewellery and secondhand finds.

Vagator

Vagator

For red cliffs, a hilltop fort and clifftop sunsets.

  • Vagator Beach — Dramatic red cliffs above a broad cove, quieter in the morning.
  • Chapora Fort — A ruined hilltop fort with wide views over the coast and the Chapora estuary.
  • Thalassa — Greek dining on a clifftop terrace; go for a sunset table.

Oxel

Oxel

For quieter northern sand, riverside villages and winter turtle nesting at Morjim.

  • Morjim Beach — A wide, quiet beach where Olive Ridley turtles nest in winter.
  • La Plage — French-leaning beach dining at Ashvem with a barefoot, easy atmosphere.
  • Siolim — A riverside village of old mansions and a quiet alternative to the coast.

Arambol

Arambol

For the far north: a long headland beach, craft stalls and yoga retreats.

  • Arambol Beach — Long headland beach with drum circles at sunset and craft stalls.
  • Sweet Water Lake — A small freshwater lake behind the beach, reached by a short forest walk.
  • Ashiyana Yoga Retreat — A long-standing retreat on the Mandrem coast with daily yoga and Ayurvedic meals.

Colva

Benaulim

For long beach days and a slower, more spacious pace in the south.

  • Colva Beach — A long, family-friendly beach with easy access and plenty of shacks.
  • Benaulim Beach — A slower southern stretch with fishermen, palms and space to walk.
  • Martin's Corner — A South Goa favourite for crab xec xec and its Goan fish curry.

Ponda

Ponda

For an inland half-day of temples, a spice-plantation walk and a restored mosque.

  • Shri Mangueshi Temple — A serene 18th-century temple with a tall deepstambha; dress modestly.
  • Sahakari Spice Farm — A guided walk through a spice plantation, ending with a Goan lunch on banana leaf.
  • Safa Shahouri Masjid — A restored 16th-century mosque with a quiet garden and a water tank.

Getting there and around

Choose a coast, then keep the transfers short.

Dabolim airport sits in Goa on the Mormugao side, as a civil enclave within a naval air base; the Airports Authority of India lists Vasco-da-Gama as its nearest railway station. Friday’s research notes also refer to Mopa as a second Goa airport, serving the north. Which suits you depends on where you are staying, so check both before booking flights.

Friday’s sample planning data suggests roughly 25–30 minutes by road between the Candolim–Calangute strip and Panjim, 35–40 minutes from Anjuna–Assagao, and 45–55 minutes from Colva–Benaulim. These are indicative: coastal traffic slows quickly in high season, so allow extra time and confirm transfers with your driver or hotel.

Practicalities and etiquette

Small things that make the days easier.

  • Dress modestly at temples, as the catalogue notes for Shri Mangueshi Temple in Ponda, and be equally considerate at the churches of Old Goa.
  • Markets keep their own days: the catalogue lists Anjuna’s flea market on Wednesdays, the Friday bazaar at Mapusa and Ingo’s Night Bazaar on Saturday evenings in Arpora. Check current timings before planning around them.
  • Morjim is described as a quiet beach where Olive Ridley turtles nest in winter, so take extra care on the sand there.
  • Beach shacks are reported to open from October to May. Confirm what is open on your dates, especially outside the dry months.
  • Popular tables such as Bomras suit booking ahead; Friday cannot make reservations for you.

Sample route shapes

How the planner’s trip lengths can be laid out.

These mirror the planner’s three lengths. They are starting shapes, not itineraries or bookings; move the days around to suit your dates and pace.

About 4 days

One stretch of coast

Base on a single beach area, such as Candolim–Calangute or Anjuna–Assagao. Give one day to Panjim and Old Goa, with Old Goa in the morning and Fontainhas in the late afternoon, and leave the rest for sand, a long lunch and one evening out.

About 7 days

North coast plus heritage

Spend most nights on the north coast, then add Panjim and Old Goa, a Vagator and Chapora Fort sunset, and an inland half-day at Ponda. Or split the week: four nights north and three in Colva–Benaulim for a quieter finish.

About 14 days

North, south and slow days

Move base once or twice: Arambol or Oxel in the far north, a village base in Anjuna–Assagao, then the quieter southern beaches. Keep a spare day or two for yoga, markets and doing very little.

Bases

North Goa or South Goa for a first stay?

There is no single best choice for every trip. Friday’s catalogue clusters lively north-coast beaches around Calangute, Anjuna and Vagator, quieter sands toward Colva and Benaulim, and heritage walks in Panjim and Old Goa. If beach access and short drives matter most, choose one coastal base; save Old Goa and Fontainhas for a focused half-day. Confirm current shack seasons, transfers and prices directly—these sample listings are not live booking details.

Quick answers

Questions we hear about Goa.

Which airport should I fly into?

Dabolim is the long-standing Goa airport, with Mopa referred to in Friday’s notes as a second airport in the north. Compare transfer times to your base before choosing, and confirm flight options directly.

Can I see north and south Goa in a few days?

You can, but it is a lot of road for a short stay. With fewer than a week, one coastal base plus a day in Panjim and Old Goa is usually calmer.

How long do Old Goa and Fontainhas take?

Friday’s planning notes suggest Old Goa for a morning and Fontainhas for a late-afternoon walk, which fits comfortably into one day from Panjim.

Is Goa worth visiting in the monsoon?

It is quieter and greener, but heavy rain and closed beach shacks change the trip. Check current weather and operating guidance, as this guide does not confirm live conditions.
